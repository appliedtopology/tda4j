# WORKLOG: lighter models than the bar construction; non-group nerves as examples (2026-10-10)

A question from the project lead, not an implementation task: (1) are there lighter constructions than the bar construction for
B of groups, monoids, categories ("and powers"); (2) are there small, interesting non-group classifying spaces to use as examples
for the `sset` machinery. No library code changed. The numbers below were computed with the library's own `Nerve`,
`ClassifyingSpace.bettiNumbers`, `SimplicialSet.fromSimplicialComplex` and `bettiNumbers(p)`, in a scratch spec that was deleted
afterwards. Every run took under 2 s.

## Verified numbers (the library, F_2 and F_3)

**Rectangular band `A x B` with an identity adjoined.** Index 0 is the identity, `(a,b) -> 1 + a*|B| + b`, and
`(a,b)(c,d) = (a,d)`. `validateMonoid()` and `skeleton(3).validate()` were both clean. Betti numbers in degrees 0..4 (0..3 for 3x3 and 2x4):

| band | F_2 | F_3 |
|---|---|---|
| 2x2 | 1,0,1,0,0 | 1,0,1,0,0 |
| 2x3 | 1,0,2,0,0 | 1,0,2,0,0 |
| 3x3 | 1,0,4,0 | 1,0,4,0 |
| 2x4 | 1,0,3,0 | 1,0,3,0 |

These agree with B ≃ a wedge of (|A|-1)(|B|-1) copies of S^2. The 2x2 case is Fiedorowicz 1984 and the general case is in Steinberg,
"The homology of completely simple semigroups", arXiv:2405.06594, Thm 3.1. A nontrivial finite group never shows this pattern:
there, positive-degree homology is torsion and is nonzero in infinitely many degrees.

**Rees matrix monoid `M[Z/2; 2, 2; P]^1`.** Elements are `(i, g, λ)`, indexed `1 + (2i + g)*2 + λ`. The product is
`(i,g,λ)(j,h,μ) = (i, g + P(λ)(j) + h, μ)`. Degrees 0..3:

| P | F_2 | F_3 |
|---|---|---|
| all 0 | 1,1,2,1 | 1,0,1,0 |
| [[0,0],[0,1]] | 1,0,1,1 | 1,0,1,0 |

Both rows were predicted from Steinberg's Theorem A before the run, and both matched. That theorem gives H_1 = coker ψ,
H_2 = H_2(G) ⊕ ker ψ and H_n = H_n(G) for n >= 3, where ψ: Z[Ā×B̄] -> G_ab sends (a,b) to the normalized sandwich entry. The two
monoids have the same size and the same group and differ in a single sandwich entry, and F_2 separates them. That makes this a
discriminating oracle.

**p-subgroup posets (order complexes through `fromSimplicialComplex`).** The groups were S_4, A_5 = <(012),(01234)>, and
GL(3,2) as permutations of the 7 nonzero vectors of F_2^3 (order 168 checked). S_2 = all nontrivial 2-subgroups, enumerated as
subgroups generated by at most two 2-elements; that suffices because the Sylow 2-subgroups here have order at most 8. A_2 = the
elementary abelian ones.

| group | poset | points | f-vector | Betti (F_2 = F_3) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S_4 | S_2 | 19 (9, 7, 3 of orders 2, 4, 8) | 19,39,21 | 1,0,0 |
| S_4 | A_2 | 13 | 13,12 | 1,0 |
| A_5 | S_2 = A_2 | 20 | 20,15 | 5,0 |
| GL(3,2) | S_2 | 77 (21, 35, 21) | 77,231,147 | 1,8,0 |
| GL(3,2) | A_2 | 35 | 35,42 | 1,8 |

Consistency checks on these values:
- S_4 is contractible because O_2(S_4) ≠ 1 (Quillen).
- A_5 = PSL(2,4) has a building of 5 points.
- GL(3,2) gives 8 = 2^3 = the rank of the Steinberg module. Its building (the Fano incidence graph, 14 vertices and 21 edges) is a third
  model, smaller still.
- S_p ≃ A_p (Quillen) is visible here: 77 -> 35 points, same homology.

## Literature found (via WebSearch/WebFetch, secondary summaries only)

- McDuff, Topology 18 (1979): every connected space is weakly equivalent to BM for a discrete monoid M. The monoids are infinite.
- Fiedorowicz's conjecture: every finite simply connected complex is ≃ BS for a finite semigroup S. Martinian–Steinberg,
  arXiv:2511.12657 (2025), realize finite wedges of simply connected Moore spaces this way. That paper reports the general
  conjecture as open; I did not confirm this anywhere else.
- Brown, "The geometry of rewriting systems" (1992): a collapsing scheme (= a discrete Morse matching) on the nerve of a monoid
  with a complete rewriting system. It leaves one cell per Anick chain.
- Day–Nakamura, arXiv:2401.00345: a 3-skeleton of a K(S_n,1), proved correct by Brown's method.

## Reasoning recorded (not verified computationally)

- **The nerve of a group is a minimal Kan complex.** It is 2-coskeletal, so an n-simplex with n >= 2 is determined by its boundary,
  and two edges are homotopic rel endpoints only if equal. So no Kan model of BG has fewer non-degenerate simplices. A smaller
  model must be non-Kan, a CW complex, or chain-level.
- **When a Morse collapse of the bar complex keeps the subgroup filtration.** It does if no matched pair straddles two filtration
  values. With Brown's scheme, a matched pair splits a normal-form word into a prefix and a suffix, or concatenates two words whose
  product is irreducible. So a sufficient condition is that the normal form of every element of H_j uses only H_j's letters. A pc
  presentation refining a subnormal series satisfies this. The S_4 chain in `DESIGN-persistent-group-cohomology.md` is not
  subnormal at its last step (D_8 is not normal in S_4). UNTESTED. The smallest test: Z/4 with Z/2 < Z/4 (pc rules g1^2 -> g2,
  g2^2 -> 1, g2 g1 -> g1 g2), comparing barcodes against the bar complex.
- **Resolutions (HAP style) turn the inclusion of a subgroup into a chain map, not a subcomplex.** Persistence along a chain then
  becomes the barcode of a sequence of linear maps, which is what HAP's `PersistentHomologyOfSubGroupSeries` computes, not a
  filtered stream.
