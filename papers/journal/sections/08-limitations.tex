\section{Limitations and future work}\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Speed.}

behind ripser.cpp, and behind RedZeD on the cases where it is faster. In degree three and above the gap \TODO{widens or narrows}.}
The principle that every engine is generic over the field and returns a representative for every bar has a price,
and the evaluation reports it. Where a use needs only bars over $\Ftwo$ of a Vietoris--Rips complex, a specialist is the better
tool, and the library's file formats exist to hand the problem to one.

\paragraph{Memory.}
The generic engines hold every cell of the stream and reduce over dense numbers; they are for complexes that are not Vietoris--Rips
complexes and for moderate sizes. The packed engines are lean, but a very large Vietoris--Rips complex still bounds what can be
computed more than time does.

\paragraph{Fast engines.}
The union-find engines return cycles only; they need a triangulated region (a cubical grid of dimension two or more, or a
Delaunay triangulation in which every facet has at most two cofaces, which Helix does not always give in high dimension); and their
middle degrees fall back to cohomology. We have not proved that their representatives are volume-optimal (\cref{conj:volume}).

\paragraph{Alpha complexes in high dimension.}
An untruncated dual-quadratic-programming computation stays approximate for simplices whose circumradius is far above the point
spacing, so a radius should be given; the triangulation backends build the whole complex whatever the radius. Which backend
wins depends on the ambient dimension, the number of points and the degree cap, and the rule that \code{Auto} applies was
derived from the measurements of \cref{sec:eval-alpha} for random points and is untested on clustered clouds.
\VERIFY{notes say: caps between four and $d-2$, $n\ge100$ and clustered clouds are unmeasured}

\paragraph{Exact coefficients.}
Rational coefficients are available and can blow up in size; floating-point coefficients are available with a tolerance
and are not recommended for torsion. Integer coefficients are not provided.

\paragraph{Scope.}
Multiparameter persistence, extended persistence, vineyards and periodic boundary conditions are outside the library's current scope.
Persistent group homology does not scale like HAP's resolution-based computation.

\paragraph{Language and platform.}
Every user file needs the \code{experimental.modularity} import, and the library needs Scala~3.9 and Java~21. The Scala.js build
is single-threaded, has no file system in a browser, and is slower than the JVM build by the factors of \cref{sec:eval-js}.
\VERIFY{MATLAB: the facade has not been run in MATLAB as of the plan of 2026-10-04; and the Python package is not on PyPI as of 2026-11-01}

\paragraph{Evidence.}
Where the evaluation compares with a tool on a case that tool was designed for, the other tool wins by construction; where it
compares on one that only \tdaj{} supports, there is nothing to compare with. We have tried to make each table's question
clear; the threats to validity of \cref{sec:threats} list the rest. That GUDHI's witness complexes are the same objects as
ours is not established, so the witness rows are findings and not checks.

\paragraph{Future work.}
Proving or refuting volume-optimality of the dual union-find representatives, and comparing them with HomCloud's on inputs
with distinct values; a vertex-valued cubical input, so that the $V$-construction needs no refinement; block-parallel reduction on alpha
complexes; a cursor for the cohomology engines; and a comparison of group homology with HAP on the same subgroup chains.
