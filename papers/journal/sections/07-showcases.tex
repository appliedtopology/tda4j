\section{Worked examples}\label{sec:showcases}

The examples below are chosen to exercise what the benchmark does not: representatives, torsion, relative persistence, and the
constructive layer. Each corresponds to a tutorial in the documentation, whose numbers are asserted by tests.

\subsection{Counting curves in a Truchet tiling}\label{sec:truchet}

A Truchet tile is a square with two quarter-circle arcs; a random tiling of a grid joins them into curves, some of which close
into loops while others run off the edge of the picture. Take the image whose pixel values are the distance to the nearest arc, and
its sublevel filtration. The homology of the curves alone sees only the loops. Relative to the boundary of the picture,
a curve running from edge to edge becomes a cycle too, so the long bars of the relative persistent homology are \emph{all} the
curves, open and closed. Persistence cannot tell the two kinds apart; the representatives can: a loop's representative closes
up in the interior, an arc's ends lie on the boundary. The example uses three pieces of the library together: relative
persistence (a quotient by the frame, as a cell type), a trick that places the image in a frame so that the fast cubical engine
applies, and representatives from the dual union-find engine at the scale of a large image
(\cref{sec:dual-uf}). It also exposed the tie rule of that engine: on a plateau of equal values the smaller component must die,
and a version that broke ties the other way was correct but \TODOnum{factor} slower on a $1026^2$ image.

\subsection{Distinguishing spaces that Betti numbers cannot}\label{sec:telling}

The torus and the Klein bottle have the same Betti numbers over $\Ftwo$ and different ones over $\mathbb{F}_3$: torsion shows up
only by comparing fields, which is why the default is not $\Ftwo$. The torus and the space $S^1\vee S^1\vee S^2$ have the same
Betti numbers in every field; the cup product on $H^1$, computed on the simplicial sets, separates them. The complex projective plane
and $S^2\vee S^4$ have the same Betti numbers and are separated by the Steenrod square $\mathrm{Sq}^2$ on $H^2$ (the library
also builds the mapping cone of the Hopf map, a second model of the projective plane). None of this is persistent homology: it is the
constructive layer of \cref{sec:breadth} working with the engines, and the classical results provide the oracles (a computed
cup product, or Steenrod square, that disagrees with theory is a bug).

\subsection{Circular coordinates from landmarks}\label{sec:circ}

Circular coordinates assign every data point an angle that varies continuously around a persistent loop. On a point cloud of
thousands of points the dense construction does not scale; on a landmark Dowker complex, the nerve of the landmarks restricted to
the data, it does, following Perea~\cite{perea2018multiscale}. The example in the library takes a periodic signal's
sliding-window embedding, computes the coordinate on every point from a few landmarks, and checks it against the true phase.
One sign convention in the sparse construction (the cocycle is minus the coboundary of the potential) was wrong at first and
was caught by a continuity specification: the coordinate must not jump around the loop.

\subsection{Cyclooctane: torsion and high ambient dimension}\label{sec:cyclooctane}

The conformations of cyclooctane form a data set of 6040 points in $\R^{24}$~\cite{martin2010cyclooctane}, whose conformation space is
known to be a sphere and a Klein bottle glued along two circles, a space with torsion in its homology. A full Delaunay triangulation
in $\R^{24}$ is out of reach, so the alpha complex is computed by the dual quadratic program, and the Vietoris--Rips complex by Ripser's
algorithm to a cutoff; the data is the file JavaPlex ships for its tutorial. The example exercises the odd-prime
default (a field in which the torsion is visible), DQP alpha in high ambient dimension, and representatives that locate the
Klein-bottle part of the data.

\subsection{Persistent group homology}\label{sec:group}

A chain of subgroups $H_0\le H_1\le\dots\le G$ gives a chain of classifying spaces, and so a filtered simplicial set whose
persistent homology follows the group's homology as the group grows. The library computes this for small groups, for example a
chain of subgroups of the symmetric group $S_4$, with representatives, optionally on the Brown-collapsed complex whose
critical cells are Anick chains generated directly. HAP computes the same barcode from resolutions and goes much
further; we include the example as a demonstration of the constructive layer, and we say in the documentation that it is not
the best way to compute group cohomology.
