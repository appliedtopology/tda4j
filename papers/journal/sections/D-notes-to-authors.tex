\subsection{Questions that only the authors can answer}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em, itemsep=3pt]
\item \textbf{Where does the preprint end and the edge-block material begin?} The preprint~\cite{bauer2024generating}, as arXiv
  serves it, has the three-case theorem, the first-longest-edge metadata, the in-order stream, the Dory-style coboundary lists and
  experiments inside Ripser. It does not mention blocks, lens graphs, block-keyed encodings or parallel reduction. The draft
  attributes the first group to the preprint (P) and the second to this paper (N). Is there a newer version or a second paper that carries the block
  material? Do the mirrored vertex order and the tie-run handling count as the preprint's or as ours? Is the apparent-pair test from the block's edge
  Proposition~5 as intended?
\item \textbf{Helix.} The scaladoc cites IEEE document 10917453, which the project's notes say could not be read. Who designed the
  frontier-walk algorithm: a thesis, an earlier library, or us from the paper's abstract? \Cref{sec:alpha} says nothing about its source.
\item \textbf{Barcode algebra.} Image, kernel and cokernel of maps between barcodes (Nico Stucki): which paper or earlier code?
\item \textbf{The dual union-find claim.} Prove \cref{conj:volume} before the paper (that our representative is the boundary of the
  complement component at birth), then compare with HomCloud on inputs with distinct values? Without it the claim is Flash Cubical's idea carried to
  the $T$-construction, any field and Delaunay complexes, with representatives. Is the middle-degree hybrid different enough from Flash Cubical's 3D case to count?
\item \textbf{Persistent group homology through Brown's collapse.} Is it in HAP, Ellis--King or the rewriting-systems literature? The plan of
  2026-10-04 called the uncollapsed version a tutorial rather than a contribution; the draft keeps it as a worked example.
\item \textbf{Prior-art checks not run:} simplex-index width beyond 63 bits (Dory, giotto-ph); lazy representatives from reduction
  logs (how ripser.py stores cocycles); a full persistence library with representatives in a browser; block-parallel reduction
  (Morozov--Nigmetov, Ripser++, giotto-ph); whether any earlier benchmark checked agreement of outputs at scale (the claim of
  \cref{sec:agreement} is made only inside the library's own context until this is done).
\item \textbf{Venue and shape.} The plan of 2026-10-04 recommends the Journal of Applied and Computational Topology as a system-and-methods paper; ACM TOMS
  fits better if the correctness methodology is to be the spine; a JOSS paper is a cheap add-on. The draft is plain \code{article}, so it ports
  to either; the abstract and introduction would change with the choice.
\item \textbf{Authors and the AI statement.} The author list is the one in \code{CITATION.cff}. The statement of \cref{sec:availability}
  draws on \code{CONTRIBUTORS.md}; the venue's policy and whether to name the tools need checking.
\item \textbf{Cuts.} If the paper has to be shorter, the natural cuts are the appendix catalogue, \cref{sec:group} and the
  simplicial-set part of \cref{sec:breadth}, and the rarer-complexes table; the engine family, the representatives, the correctness section and the VR,
  cubical and alpha evaluations are the spine.
\item \textbf{Defects in references.} Report the three defects of \cref{sec:references} upstream before submission? The project's catalog also mentions a
  defect in \code{jvptree} 0.3.0, for which the notes I read give no details; it is not in the draft.
\end{enumerate}

\end{itemize}
