\section{Background and related software}\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Persistent (co)homology in one page}\label{sec:ph}

A \emph{filtered complex} is a finite set of cells $\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_N$ with a filtration value $f(\sigma)\in\R$ and an
order in which every cell comes after the cells of its boundary and after every cell of smaller value. The cells of
dimension $k$ span the chain group $C_k$ over a field $\mathbb{K}$, and the boundary matrix $\partial$ records
$\partial\colon C_k\to C_{k-1}$. The \emph{standard algorithm}~\cite{elz2002,zomorodian2005computing} reduces $\partial$ from
left to right, adding earlier columns to later ones until no two nonzero columns have the same lowest nonzero row (the
\emph{pivot}), and reads the barcode off the pivots: a column with pivot $\sigma$ pairs the cell $\tau$ that owns the column
with $\sigma$, and gives a bar $[f(\sigma),f(\tau))$ in degree $\dim\sigma$; a cell that is neither a pivot nor owns a
nonzero column gives an essential bar. The same reduction, recording the column operations, produces for each bar a
\emph{representative}: for a finite bar $(\sigma,\tau)$ the reduced column of $\tau$, a cycle whose youngest cell is $\sigma$,
and for an essential bar a cycle that is not a boundary.

Four ideas make reduction fast enough for point clouds of thousands of points, and they recur throughout the paper.
\emph{Clearing}~\cite{chen2011twist,bkr2014chunks}: a cell that is a pivot cannot own a nonzero column, so its column is
skipped. \emph{Cohomology}~\cite{desilva2011dualities}: reducing the coboundary matrix, in reversed order, gives the same
barcode and, with clearing, far fewer column operations for Vietoris--Rips complexes. \emph{Implicit matrices}: Ripser stores no
matrix, but enumerates coboundaries from a combinatorial number system~\cite{bauer2021ripser}. \emph{Apparent and emergent
pairs}: columns whose pivot can be read off from the cell without any addition are paired without being reduced
\cite{bauer2021ripser}. A cohomology computation returns cocycle representatives; cycle representatives come from
a second pass, the \emph{involution}, which \v{C}ufar and Virk describe for Vietoris--Rips complexes~\cite{cufar2021involuted}
and which \cref{sec:representatives} generalises.

Three observations from the table shape what follows. First, no tool in the table combines the breadth of the toolkits with a
representative for every bar from every engine \VERIFY{claim about other tools, from their documentation as read for the table; check against the pinned versions}: Ripserer.jl and OAT come closest, and they are the natural points of
comparison for the cost of representatives (\cref{sec:eval-reps}). Second, the fast cubical tools are limited to two- and
three-dimensional images over $\Ftwo$ and return no representatives, which leaves room for a dual union-find engine
that is generic over the field, the dimension and the complex (\cref{sec:dual-uf}). Third, JavaPlex is the only tool in the table
reachable from MATLAB and generic over fields, cell types and filtrations, and it is the library \tdaj{} replaces; it is
included in the benchmark as the predecessor, not as a competitor.

\paragraph{What \tdaj{} does not try to be.}
It does not try to beat the specialists on their own ground. The design principle behind it is the opposite of a speed
record: every engine must be generic over the field and return a representative for every bar, and an optimisation that
gives up either is probably not worth having. The evaluation (\cref{sec:evaluation}) therefore reports the price of that
principle, bar for bar, against tools that do not pay it.
