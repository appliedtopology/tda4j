tda4j/Developer's Guide for TDA4j/Nerves and group homology

This page covers the part of the sset add-on built on infinite simplicial sets: nerves of monoids, groups and finite categories, persistent group homology, and Brown's collapse, which makes group homology computable in higher degrees. The finite simplicial-set machinery underneath is in the simplicial-sets section of Architecture. Everything here is in org.appliedtopology.tda4j.sset.

Lazy simplicial sets and their persistence

SimplicialSet[G] (SimplicialSet.scala) is the lazy, possibly infinite trait: generators(n) and faces are produced on demand, and skeleton(n) materializes dimensions 0..n as a FiniteSimplicialSet. A skeleton's homology is correct only below degree n.

x.persistentHomology(filtrationValue, maxDegree, prime) runs the chunks engine on the (maxDegree + 1)-skeleton, so it is exact in every degree it reports, for an infinite set too. It returns a PersistenceDiagram with a representative on every bar. A filtration that lets a generator enter before one of its faces is rejected, naming the faces.

Monoids, groups and their nerves

FiniteMonoid and its subclass FiniteGroup (FiniteGroup.scala) are multiplication tables over 0 until order.

The companions build the examples:

  • monoids: rectangularBand and reesMatrix, completely simple semigroups with an identity adjoined;
  • groups: cyclic, symmetric, alternating, generalLinear(n, p) (acting on the nonzero vectors), permutationGroup and product.

A group finds its own subgroups:

  • subgroupGeneratedBy;
  • pSubgroups(p), every nontrivial p-subgroup;
  • isElementaryAbelian, which picks out Quillen's poset.

Nerve(monoid, subset) (ClassifyingSpace.scala) is the nerve of a monoid as a one-object category. Its non-degenerate n-simplices are the tuples [x_1|...|x_n] of non-identity elements (NerveSimplex):

  • d_0 drops the first entry and d_n the last;
  • an inner face multiplies two neighbouring entries, and is degenerate when the product is the identity.

For a group this is BG; subset restricts it to BH for a subgroup H.

Finite categories

FiniteCategory (FiniteCategory.scala) is a trait with six members: the two counts, source, target, identityOf and andThen. Composition is diagrammatic: andThen(f, g) is "first f, then g", matching FiniteMonoid.multiply(a, b). validate() checks the category laws.

The constructors are:

  • fromMonoid and fromPoset;
  • freeOnAcyclicQuiver;
  • actionGroupoid and homotopyOrbits.

homotopyOrbits is the Grothendieck construction of a group acting on a simplicial complex. It returns a HomotopyOrbitCategory, whose nerve is homotopy equivalent to the Borel construction, and whose morphismsOver(H) gives the subcategory of a subgroup's action.

CategoryNerve(category) is the lazy nerve. Its non-degenerate simplices (CategorySimplex) are chains of composable non-identity morphisms, with the same face rule as Nerve. filtrationBy(chain) filters by a chain of subcategories. A poset's nerve is finite (its order complex); a category with a non-identity endomorphism has generators in every dimension.

Trap: B(M^op) is homotopy equivalent to BM, so a composition order that is flipped by mistake passes every Betti-number check. Test a nerve cell for cell and face for face against Nerve, and a poset's nerve by its exact f-vector against the order complex.

Persistent group homology

ClassifyingSpace.persistentGroupHomology(g, chain, maxDegree, prime) computes H_n(H_i; F_p) as H_i runs up an increasing chain of subgroups:

  • a simplex enters with the first subgroup containing all its entries (ClassifyingSpace.filtrationBy);
  • births and deaths are positions in the chain;
  • every bar's representative is a cycle of BG.

bettiNumbers(g, maxDegree, prime) reads the essential classes of the one-step chain.

The call picks its complex:

  • when RewritingSystem.refining(g, chain) succeeds, it runs on Brown's collapse;
  • otherwise it runs on the bar complex, filtered.

The barcode is the same either way. The bar complex has (|G| - 1)^n cells in dimension n, so the collapse is what makes degrees above 2 or 3 reachable.

Brown's collapse

Brown's collapsing scheme ("The geometry of rewriting systems", 1992) turns a complete rewriting system for a monoid into a discrete Morse matching on its bar complex. The cells left unmatched (critical) are the Anick chains, with a Morse boundary of their own and the same homology.

RewritingSystem (CollapsedNerve.scala) gives the normal form of every element and a level for every letter. A rule must decrease in the collection order, the wreath product of shortlex orders by level, and validate() checks this. Shortlex alone would reject the rules of a polycyclic presentation, which lengthen words.

RewritingSystem.refining(group, chain) builds such a system: a series of prime-index normal steps from 1 to G passing through every subgroup of the chain, with one letter per step. It returns Left, naming the step, when a subgroup is not subnormal in the next or a quotient is not solvable. That is always possible for a p-group, and never for A_5 or S_5.

CollapsedNerve(rewriting):

  • criticalCells(n) generates the Anick chains directly; the bar complex is never enumerated;
  • morseBoundary computes boundaries by a memoized flow over the matching;
  • lift maps a critical cell back to a bar-complex chain, so representatives come back as cycles of BG;
  • stream(chain, maxDegree) gives the filtered critical cells for the chunks engine or the verb (import collapsed.given);
  • persistentHomology(chain, maxDegree, prime) returns the lifted diagram.

The flow runs over an explicit stack. A cell found again while still in progress means the matching is not acyclic, and the flow throws rather than loops. Coefficients are exact Longs, and an overflow throws with a message naming the bar complex as the way out.

Trap: filter only by a chain that the rewriting system respects, meaning each subgroup's normal forms use only its own letters. A matching that straddles two levels still gives a monotone filtration, but the barcode is wrong. stream and persistentHomology check this and refuse.

Limits: the flow's memo grows exponentially with the degree, more slowly than the bar complex but enough to be the limit for larger groups. There are no cup products or Steenrod squares on the collapsed complex.

Testing: BrownCollapseReference in the test tree classifies every bar cell and runs the unmemoized flow. CollapsedNerveSpec compares the two term for term, and compares the barcodes with the bar complex's over F_2 and F_3. Keep both whenever the collapse changes.
