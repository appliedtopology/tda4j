tda4j/User Guide for TDA4j/The persistent Laplacian

The persistent Laplacian of a filtered complex is a symmetric matrix, one for each dimension k and each pair of filtration values from <= to. Its kernel has the dimension of the persistent Betti number: the number of classes of dimension k in the complex at from that are still classes in the complex at to, which is what the barcode counts. Its other eigenvalues are what the barcode leaves out: how well connected the classes are, so how much a small change of the complex can do to them. The smallest eigenvalue that is not zero, the spectral gap, is the usual summary of that.

import scala.language.experimental.modularity
import org.appliedtopology.tda4j.*

val square = Array(Array(0.0, 0.0), Array(1.0, 0.0), Array(1.0, 1.0), Array(0.0, 1.0))
val complex = VietorisRips(EuclideanMetricSpace(square), maxDimension = 1)

val loop = PersistentLaplacian(complex, dimension = 1, from = 1.0, to = 1.0)
loop.bettiNumber    // 1: the loop of the four sides
loop.eigenvalues    // 0, 2, 2, 4
loop.spectralGap    // Some(2.0)

val filled = PersistentLaplacian(complex, dimension = 1, from = 1.0, to = math.sqrt(2))
filled.bettiNumber  // 0: the diagonals enter and the loop is a boundary

PersistentLaplacian(stream, dimension, from, to) takes any stream of cells, a Vietoris-Rips complex or a stream you built, and returns a result with cells (the simplices at from, the rows and columns of the matrix), matrix, eigenvalues (smallest first), bettiNumber and spectralGap. The stream has to hold the cells one dimension above dimension: a Vietoris-Rips complex built with maxDimension = k serves dimensions up to k.

What it is

Take the chains of the complex K_to whose boundary lies in the complex K_from, and the boundary map from them into the chains of K_from. With the cells as an orthonormal basis, that map has an adjoint, and the persistent Laplacian is the map composed with its adjoint, plus the down Laplacian ∂ᵀ∂ of K_from. When nothing enters between from and to it is the Hodge Laplacian ∂∂ᵀ + ∂ᵀ∂. The coefficients are real numbers, and the matrices are dense: dimensions up to a few thousand cells are fine, which is what a Vietoris-Rips complex of a few dozen points has.

The chains to take are those with a boundary in K_from, not all of K_to: a chain through a simplex that enters after from has no boundary there. That is the difference from the Laplacian of K_to restricted to the cells of K_from, which has a kernel of the wrong size.

From the other interfaces

interface call
MATLAB and Java TDA4j.persistentLaplacianFromPoints(points, dimension, from, to) and ...FromDistanceMatrix: a PersistentLaplacianResult with matrix(), eigenvalues(), bettiNumber(), spectralGap() (NaN if there is none; hasSpectralGap()), cells()
JavaScript persistentLaplacian(points, dimension, from, to) and persistentLaplacianFromDistances: an object with matrix, eigenvalues, bettiNumber, spectralGap and cells
Python tda4j.persistent_laplacian(points, dimension, start, stop) and persistent_laplacian_from_distances: the same attributes, betti_number and spectral_gap (None if all are zero)
Command line tda4j --persistent-laplacian --laplacian-dimension 1 --laplacian-from 1 --laplacian-to 1 points.csv
